Trusts, Wills & Estates

Delaware estate planning and estate administration guidance for wills, trusts, powers of attorney, living wills, probate, and incapacity planning.

Hudson Jones Jaywork & Fisher helps Delaware clients prepare wills, consider trusts, plan for incapacity, and address estate administration. Whether you are making a first estate plan, revisiting existing documents, or handling a loved one's estate, contact the firm to discuss your circumstances and the appropriate attorney.

Wills and Estate Planning

A will can address beneficiaries, specific gifts, an executor, and guardianship wishes for minor children. The firm's estate-planning work includes Last Wills and Testaments, beneficiary planning, and charitable bequests. Bring existing documents and explain the family or financial changes that prompted your visit so counsel can discuss what needs attention.

Trust Planning

The firm advises on revocable living trusts, irrevocable trusts, special needs trusts, testamentary trusts, and charitable trusts where appropriate. The suitable approach depends on your goals, assets, and family circumstances. Discuss the purpose of a proposed trust with counsel rather than assuming that every estate plan requires one.

Powers of Attorney and Health Care Directives

Planning also includes decisions about who may act if you become unable to manage financial, legal, or medical matters. The firm prepares durable powers of attorney, advance health-care directives, health-care powers of attorney, and living wills. These documents address different decisions and should be considered together with your broader plan.

Probate and Estate Administration

The firm assists with probate, estate administration, and fiduciary and beneficiary questions. If you have been named an executor or need help with an estate, gather the available will, trust documents, correspondence, and asset information for an initial discussion. Counsel can explain which records and next steps apply to the particular matter.

Delaware Estate Planning Questions

Q.What can a will address?

A will can name beneficiaries and an executor, address specific gifts, and express guardianship wishes for minor children. Discuss your family and assets with counsel when preparing or reviewing a will.

Q.Does every estate plan need a trust?

The appropriate documents depend on your circumstances and objectives. The firm advises on several types of trusts where appropriate; an attorney can discuss whether a trust belongs in your plan.

Q.Can I discuss powers of attorney and health care directives?

Yes. The firm's services include durable powers of attorney, advance health-care directives, health-care powers of attorney, and living wills.

Q.Who can I contact about probate or estate administration?

Contact the firm to discuss the estate and available records. Katrina K. Doran's profile includes estate administration, and Christopher M. Holmon's profile includes wills and probate. The firm can help connect you with the appropriate attorney.
