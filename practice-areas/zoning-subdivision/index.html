Hudson, Jones, Jaywork & Fisher represents Delaware governmental entities, property owners, developers, and businesses in zoning, subdivision, land use, and development matters. Zoning and subdivision laws shape communities and affect property values, development opportunities, and quality of life.

For municipalities and governmental entities, the firm assists with zoning ordinances, subdivision regulations, comprehensive plans, planning commissions, boards of adjustment, land-use procedures, and defense of zoning decisions or ordinances. The firm also advises on public meetings, open meeting requirements, decision-making standards, and legal requirements affecting land-use approvals.

For private clients, the firm assists with zoning due diligence, variance applications, special exception and conditional use applications, subdivision approvals, annexation, rezoning petitions, development agreements, appeals from zoning board decisions, declaratory judgment actions, injunctions, and eminent domain or condemnation matters in Kent County, Sussex County, and other Delaware jurisdictions.

Delaware Zoning and Land Use FAQ

Q.What is a zoning variance in Delaware?

A variance is a request for permission to use or develop property in a way that differs from the strict terms of a zoning rule. The standards and process depend on the local jurisdiction and the type of variance requested.

Q.Who hears Delaware zoning and subdivision applications?

Applications may be heard by planning commissions, boards of adjustment, town councils, county councils, or other local bodies depending on the property location and requested approval.

Q.Can zoning decisions be appealed?

Some Delaware zoning and land-use decisions may be appealed, but deadlines and procedures can be short. Property owners, developers, and municipalities should review appeal rights promptly.
