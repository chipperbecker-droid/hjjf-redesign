Condominium and Homeowners Association Law

Legal counsel for Delaware association boards and property managers on assessments, governing documents, governance, and community disputes.

Counsel for Association Boards

A homeowner falls behind on assessments. A board needs to update its bylaws. An owner challenges a rule or a maintenance decision. Each issue calls for a careful review of the community's documents and the law that applies to it.

Hudson, Jones, Jaywork & Fisher advises Delaware homeowners associations and condominium associations on governance, assessment collection, governing documents, and community disputes. The firm works with boards and property managers from its Dover and Rehoboth Beach offices.

Assessments and Collections

Unpaid assessments can put pressure on a community's budget. The firm helps associations review their collection options, including demand letters, payment plans, settlement agreements, liens, and litigation. Foreclosure or other enforcement may be available where appropriate, depending on the governing documents and applicable law.

Collection matters require attention to the association's records, its authority to act, and applicable debt collection and consumer protection requirements. Counsel can help the board evaluate a proposed next step before taking action.

Governing Documents

Declarations, bylaws, covenants, deed restrictions, and rules guide how a community operates. The firm drafts, amends, interprets, and restates these documents, and advises boards on questions about enforcement and community responsibilities.

Whether the issue involves a proposed amendment or a disagreement over existing language, the starting point is the community's own documents. A change suitable for one association may not be appropriate for another.

Board Governance and DUCIOA

The firm advises on board meetings, elections, developer transitions, reserve funding, insurance obligations, and maintenance responsibilities. It also helps associations address questions under the Delaware Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act, commonly called DUCIOA.

DUCIOA's application depends on the community and the issue. Boards should seek advice specific to their association rather than assume that a rule applies the same way to every Delaware HOA or condominium.

Association Disputes

Disagreements can involve assessments, rule enforcement, maintenance, or the meaning of governing documents. The firm assists associations with evaluating the dispute and considering available options. Matters requiring court proceedings may also involve the firm's civil litigation services.

For related property transactions, title questions, or other property matters, explore the firm's Delaware real estate services.

Prepare for a Conversation

Useful starting materials may include:

  • The declaration, bylaws, rules, and relevant amendments
  • A short timeline and description of the board's question
  • Relevant meeting minutes, notices, and correspondence
  • Assessment records or maintenance documents related to the issue

Begin with a brief description of the matter. Ask the firm how to provide records before sending confidential documents.

Delaware HOA Questions

Q.What legal issues do Delaware associations face?

Common issues include assessment collection, covenant enforcement, governing document interpretation, board meetings and elections, maintenance responsibilities, developer transitions, and owner disputes.

Q.What is DUCIOA?

DUCIOA is the Delaware Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act. It addresses common-interest communities, but its application depends on the community and the issue. Counsel can review the relevant documents and circumstances. The Delaware Code provides the text of the Act.

Q.Can an association collect unpaid assessments?

An association may have options such as demand letters, payment plans, liens, litigation, or other enforcement measures. The available approach depends on its governing documents, the facts, and applicable law.

Q.Can the firm help update bylaws and rules?

The firm drafts, amends, interprets, and restates association governing documents. Counsel can help a board review proposed language and the requirements that apply to making a change.

Q.Does this page address association representation?

Yes. This page describes services for association boards and property managers. An individual owner seeking advice about a dispute can contact the firm to ask whether representation is available; the firm will need to review the matter and potential conflicts.
