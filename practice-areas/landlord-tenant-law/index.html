Landlord/Tenant Law

Delaware landlord-tenant counsel for commercial and residential lease disputes, possession actions, rent issues, security deposits, and housing compliance.

On this page

  1. Overview
  2. Questions
  3. Attorneys

Hudson, Jones, Jaywork & Fisher represents landlords, tenants, property owners, and property managers in Delaware residential and commercial landlord-tenant matters. Whether a disagreement concerns a lease, unpaid rent, a security deposit, property conditions, or possession of a rental, the firm helps clients review the documents and circumstances and consider their options.

For Landlords and Property Managers

The firm's existing practice includes summary possession actions, non-payment of rent disputes, holdover tenancies, lease breaches, security deposit issues, and property damage claims. An attorney may review the lease, notices, payment history, and supporting records to evaluate the matter and the available approach.

The firm also assists with residential and commercial lease drafting, review, and interpretation. Clear lease terms can help identify the parties' obligations and address questions before they become contested issues. For property transactions, title questions, and other property matters, see our Delaware real estate practice.

For Tenants

The firm represents tenants in matters involving eviction defense, security deposit recovery, habitability, lease interpretation, landlord harassment, entry concerns, and lease negotiations or renewals. The relevant issues depend on the property, the agreement, the communications between the parties, and the circumstances of the dispute.

If you have received a notice or court papers, tell the firm about any scheduled hearing or response date when you contact the office. The firm can discuss whether it can assist with your particular matter.

Rent, Deposits, and Lease Disputes

Rental disputes often turn on the records: what the lease says, which payments were made, what condition the property was in, and what each party communicated. The firm helps clients evaluate disagreements over rent, holdover issues, damage claims, security deposit deductions, repairs, and other lease obligations.

Some matters may involve negotiation or other efforts to resolve the disagreement; others may involve court proceedings. The appropriate approach depends on the facts, the lease, and the applicable procedure. Our civil litigation practice provides information about the firm's broader dispute work.

Preparing to Discuss Your Matter

If available, gather the following records for an initial discussion:

  • The signed lease, amendments, renewals, and any related agreements.
  • Notices, court papers, and information about hearings or response dates.
  • A rent ledger, receipts, payment records, and security deposit documentation.
  • Move-in or move-out records, photographs, repair requests, and damage estimates.
  • Relevant correspondence and a short timeline of the disagreement.

You can contact the firm before every document is assembled. Describe the issue and ask how to provide the relevant records.

Delaware Landlord-Tenant Questions

Q.Does the firm represent landlords and tenants?

The firm's existing landlord-tenant practice includes representation of landlords, property owners, property managers, and tenants. Contact the firm to discuss whether it can assist with your specific matter.

Q.Can the firm help with a residential or commercial lease?

Yes. The practice includes residential and commercial lease drafting, review, interpretation, and disputes. Bring the lease and any amendments or renewal documents when discussing the issue.

Q.What records help explain a security deposit dispute?

The lease, deposit records, move-in and move-out documentation, photographs, receipts, damage estimates, and correspondence can help an attorney understand the disagreement. The relevance of each record depends on the facts.

Q.What should I mention if I have received court papers?

Tell the firm what papers or notices you received, when you received them, and any hearing or response date shown. Keep the papers available when you contact the office to discuss whether the firm can assist.

Q.Which attorneys handle landlord-tenant matters?

The published profiles of Michael G. Rushe and Katrina K. Doran include landlord/tenant work. Contact the firm to discuss which attorney is appropriate for your particular needs.
